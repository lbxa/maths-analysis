% Homework 03, question 2.
\begin{proof}[Solution]
  Since $\sqrt{2}<2$, we have $5-\sqrt{2}>0$. The existence and
  uniqueness of nonnegative cube roots therefore gives a number
  \[
    c=(5-\sqrt{2})^{1/3}>0,\qquad c^3=5-\sqrt{2}.
  \]
  If $a\leq0$, then $a^3\leq0<c^3$, so $a\notin A$. On the
  nonnegative real numbers, $x \mapsto x^3$ is strictly increasing. Hence
  $a^3>c^3$ holds exactly when $a>c$, and therefore
  \[
    A=(c,\infty).
  \]

  For part (a), take
  \[
    B=\R\setminus A=(-\infty,c].
  \]
  Then $A\cup B=\R$ and $A\cap B=\varnothing$. Both sets are
  nonempty, since $c+1\in A$ and $c\in B$. For every $b\in B$ and
  $a\in A$, we have
  \[
    b\leq c<a.
  \]
  Thus $B$ is the lower part and $A$ is the upper part of a Dedekind
  partition, as shown in \autoref{fig:hw03:cubic-partition}.
  \begin{marginfigure}
    \centering
    \input{figures/hw03/cubic-partition}
    \caption[A cubic Dedekind partition.]{Schematic partition with
      the boundary included in $B$ and excluded from $A$.
      The two intervals together cover the real line.}
    \label{fig:hw03:cubic-partition}
  \end{marginfigure}

  For part (b), the displayed inequality shows that $c$ is a boundary
  for this partition. To see that it is unique, suppose $d$ is another
  boundary. Since $c\in B$, we must have $c\leq d$. If $d>c$, then
  \[
    a=\frac{c+d}{2}\in A,\qquad a<d,
  \]
  contradicting the boundary inequality $d\leq a$. Thus $d=c$, so
  the Dedekind cut is $(5-\sqrt{2})^{1/3}$.

  Geometrically, the two proposed boundaries would leave $(c,d)$
  uncovered. Every element of $B$ is at most $c$, and the proposed
  boundary $d$ would require every element of $A$ to be at least $d$.
  The midpoint lies in neither region, contradicting $A\cup B=\R$,
  as illustrated in \autoref{fig:hw03:dedekind-cut-uniqueness}.
  \begin{marginfigure}
    \centering
    \input{figures/hw03/dedekind-cut-uniqueness}
    \caption[Uniqueness of a Dedekind cut.]{Schematic regions allowed by
      two proposed boundaries $c<d$. They force
      $B\subseteq(-\infty,c]$ and $A\subseteq[d,\infty)$.
      Their gap contains $(c+d)/2$, contradicting $A\cup B=\R$.}
    \label{fig:hw03:dedekind-cut-uniqueness}
  \end{marginfigure}

  For part (c), $c$ is a lower bound of $A$. Any $d>c$ fails to be a
  lower bound, because $(c+d)/2$ belongs to $A$ and is smaller than
  $d$. Hence $c$ is the greatest lower bound of $A$. Also, $c$ is an
  upper bound of $B$ and belongs to $B$, so it is its greatest element
  and therefore its least upper bound. Consequently,
  \[
    \inf A=\sup B=(5-\sqrt{2})^{1/3}.
  \]
\end{proof}
