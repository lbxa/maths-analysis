\begin{proof}[Solution]
  From the hint, we have
  \[
    (\sqrt a-\sqrt b)^2=a-2\sqrt{ab}+b.
  \]
  Since a square is nonnegative,
  \begin{align*}
    (\sqrt a-\sqrt b)^2&\ge0,\\
    a-2\sqrt{ab}+b&\ge0,\\
    a+b&\ge2\sqrt{ab},\\
    \frac{a+b}{2}&\ge\sqrt{ab}.
  \end{align*}
  Each rearrangement is reversible. Thus
  \begin{align*}
    \frac{a+b}{2}>\sqrt{ab}\quad
      &\Longleftrightarrow\quad
        (\sqrt a-\sqrt b)^2>0\\
      &\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sqrt a\ne\sqrt b\\
      &\Longleftrightarrow\quad a\ne b.
  \end{align*}
  Therefore the inequality is strict if and only if $a\ne b$.
  Equality holds exactly when $a=b$, including $a=b=0$.
\end{proof}
