\begin{proof}[Solution]
  We start by taking out a factor of $n^3$ to simplify the term inside the
  $n$th root.
  The remaining factor is positive for every $n\ge1$,
  since $5-2/n^2+100/n^3\ge5-2=3$. We first factor at each $n$ and
  take limits only after establishing convergence of both factors.
  \begin{align*}
    (5n^3-2n+100)^{1/n}
      &=\left(n^3\left(5-\frac{2}{n^2}+\frac{100}{n^3}\right)\right)^{1/n}\\
      &=(n^3)^{1/n}\left(5-\frac{2}{n^2}+\frac{100}{n^3}\right)^{1/n}.
  \end{align*}
  Since we know $\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{1/n}=1$,
  \[
    \lim_{n\to\infty}n^{3/n}
      =\lim_{n\to\infty}(n^{1/n})^3=1^3=1.
  \]

  Next for the right-side factor, the bounds below
  hold for $n\ge5$. Indeed,

  \[
    4<5-\frac{2}{25}
      \le5-\frac{2}{n^2}+\frac{100}{n^3}
      \le5+\frac{100}{125}<6.
  \]

  \begin{align*}
    4&<5-\frac{2}{n^2}+\frac{100}{n^3}<6,\\
    4^{1/n}&<\left(5-\frac{2}{n^2}+\frac{100}{n^3}\right)^{1/n}<6^{1/n}.
  \end{align*}
  Both bounds need only hold on this tail.
  The bounding sequences have limits
  \[
    \lim_{n\to\infty}4^{1/n}=1,\quad
    \lim_{n\to\infty}6^{1/n}=1.
  \]
  Then by the squeeze theorem,
  \[
    \lim_{n\to\infty}\left(5-\frac{2}{n^2}+\frac{100}{n^3}\right)^{1/n}=1.
  \]
  Both factor limits now exist, so the product rule
  applies to the factored expression.
  Hence the final limit is the product of $1\times1$.
\end{proof}
