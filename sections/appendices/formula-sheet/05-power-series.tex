\section{Power series}
\label{sec:appendix:formula-sheet-power-series}
For $\sum c_nx^n$,
\[
  R=\frac1{\limsup|c_n|^{1/n}},\qquad
  1/0=+\infty,\quad1/(+\infty)=0.
\]
If defined with a limit, $R=\lim|c_n/c_{n+1}|$ also works.
There is absolute convergence for $|x|<R$, divergence for $|x|>R$;
test $x=\pm R$ separately. At $x=0$ the series equals $c_0$.
For $a>0$, $k\in\Z$,
\[
  \left|\frac{x^n}{a^n n^k}\right|^{1/n}
     =\frac{|x|}{a(n^{1/n})^k}\longrightarrow\frac{|x|}{a}.
\]
Thus $R=a$, while $|x|/a$ is the test value, not the radius.
At $x=\pm a$, absolute convergence holds iff $k>1$ by $\sum n^{-k}$.
For $k=1$, $x=-a$ converges conditionally and $x=a$ diverges;
for $k\le0$, endpoint terms do not tend to zero.
