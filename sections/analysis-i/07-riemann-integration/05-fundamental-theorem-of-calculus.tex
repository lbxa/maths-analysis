\section{Fundamental theorem of calculus}
\label{sec:analysis-i:fundamental-theorem-of-calculus}

\begin{pendingreview}
The fundamental theorem will connect integration, which accumulates
quantities, with differentiation, which measures local change.
\historynote{Newton's \emph{De analysi} (1669) computes areas using
series and rules relating powers to quadratures~\cite{Newton1669}.
Together with the differential calculus published by Leibniz
~\cite{Leibniz1684}, this belongs to the development that made
differentiation and integration a unified method. The modern theorem
adds explicit continuity and differentiability hypotheses; its
applications include mechanics and differential equations.}
\end{pendingreview}
